**Task:** Network Diagramming (AoA)

## 1. Why is identifying the critical path important for project managers?
[cite_start]The critical path provides a visual roadmap of the project's longest sequence[cite: 49]. By drawing the AoA diagram, I realized that some activities can happen in parallel (like Design and Data Collection), but the project cannot finish until the longest sequence is done. Knowing this helps the manager prevent "resource hoarding" on tasks that have plenty of float time, like UI Development.

## 2. Which activity posed the highest scheduling risk?
**Activity A (Data Collection)** felt the riskiest to me from a dependency standpoint. [cite_start]Since it is the very first step on the critical path[cite: 55], any delay here ripples through the entire network. If we can't scrape enough facial images in the first 2 days, Activity B and C cannot start, and the whole diagram shifts to the right immediately.

## 3. What trade-offs are involved when crashing a project?
The main trade-off is **increased management complexity and risk**. Crashing often means doing things in parallel that should be sequential (fast-tracking) or adding more people to a task (crashing). Adding more people to "Data Cleaning" might speed it up, but it increases the risk of miscommunication and inconsistent data formats, which could cause bugs later.
